Spatial
sound

From headphones to the dancefloor

For about ten years, Villagomez has been recording the world around him: Pacific waves, fireworks in Lima, English trains, percussion, voices, club crowds. His research carries this material from headphones to the spatialized dancefloor, where the audience no longer just faces the sound: it's inside it.

2014 · First contact

First day of an internship at Radio-Canada: 157 microphones around a piano, for Roman Zavada's Résonances Boréales, in the SAT dome. That's where he discovers sympathetic resonance and hears the word “electroacoustic” for the first time.

Read the story

Sonic storytelling

Every piece is a journey, with a beginning, a tension and an arrival. A set is built like a story: from meditation to dance, then back to calm.

Immersive recording

Multichannel ambisonic and ultrasonic microphones, field recordings and hundreds of layers: a library of about ten years of recordings, designed for space from the moment of capture. His surroundings become raw material: from creative recording of construction materials in his father's garage to a library of club sounds at Soubois, including after-work cocktail ambiences captured without music.

Electronic and acoustic

Modular basslines and instruments played for real, Afro-Latin and Haitian percussion captured with multiple microphones. No generative AI: every sound is recorded or made by hand.

Industry standards

Research designed to serve working professionals: creation moves through several applications, and the final mix is done in sync in Pro Tools. A single work must translate from the dome to quadraphonic, binaural and stereo.

Spatial sound is the future of the dancefloor. I want to give a new voice to traditional music, not just electronic music, in a blended sound that reflects Montreal.

Villagomez

Montreal DJ, producer, sound designer and mastering engineer. Samuel Villagomez sees himself first as a performer: to him, every gesture is a musical gesture.

It all starts with a grandmother who was an opera singer and piano teacher. Violin from age four, then cello in symphony orchestras throughout his youth. He started DJing at fourteen and has been producing for about fifteen years.

A college diploma (DEC) in audiovisual electronics, then a bachelor's in digital music at Université de Montréal. There, he looks to learn live performance at a fixed tempo, the tempo of the dancefloor, or to play cello experimentally. In second year, he builds a project of metal sheets played live and looped into industrial techno. Still too conventional, according to his professors.

In 2014-2015, a three-month internship at Radio-Canada in audio post-production and music recording and mixing, alongside each of the audio technicians, all the way to a day on the Bye Bye, Radio-Canada's New Year's Eve special. On his very first day, seated at the SSL console beside sound engineer Guy Charbonneau, he watches a piano being recorded with 157 microphones, with pianist Roman Zavada and his brother Ivan Zavada, for the Résonances Boréales project, presented in the SAT dome. The two brothers were expecting him: their mother, a close friend of his grandmother, had told them he was coming. That's where he discovers sympathetic resonance, strings that start to vibrate without being struck, in response to the ones being played, and where he hears the word “electroacoustic” for the first time.

Next comes an exchange in Sonic Arts & Composition at the University of Huddersfield, in the UK, including mixing work in a near-anechoic studio. That's where he finds his voice and where his musical project is born. In 2020, he wraps up his studies with an internship at La Hacienda Creative, where he plays cello on soundtracks and comes back to his metal sheets. He has also completed three TouchDesigner trainings at the SAT.

In the field: technical direction, lighting and lasers at Soubois and La Voûte, with training on grandMA lighting consoles, 800-person nights, and more than fifty events organized since age 18, after house parties and free raves put together with friends as a teenager. As a DJ: residencies at Soubois, La Voûte, Club Pelicano, Le Balcon, Motel Motel and Terrasse Belvu; opening sets for Claptone, Black Coffee, Guy Gerber and DJ Ruckus; and stages from Hôtel W and Secret Garden by Veuve Clicquot to Cirque du Soleil, England and Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon.
